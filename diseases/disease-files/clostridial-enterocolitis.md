Caused by: Clostridium perfringens and/or Clostridium difficile

**Affects:** Horses and foals (newborn foals predominantly)

Common causes of enterocolitis in horses and foals - cannot be clinically distinguished from salmonellosis.

**Primary Organ System:** Gastrointestinal tract (intestines/colon)

**Transmission:** Clostridial spores persist in environment and are resistant to disinfectants, allowing nosocomial infection; associated with antimicrobial use, stressors, food deprivation

**Clinical Signs:**

- Bloody diarrhoea

- Colic

- Fluid- and gas-filled intestines

**Pathological signs:**

- GIT: intestinal necrosis with large Gram-positive rods seen in intestinal smear

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- C. perfringens Type A (most common) and Type C (rarely identified, but highest mortality) produce toxin A (increased vascular permeability, haemolysin) and toxin C (necrotising activity) — both lethal toxins

- C. difficile produces toxin A and/or B → fluid secretion → intestinal inflammation

**Diagnosis:**

- Anaerobic bacterial culture (lab must be informed that clostridial enterocolitis is a potential differential, as anaerobic culture is not routine)
  - Samples: blood (if severe enterocolitis), fresh faecal samples, intestinal contents — must be collected as soon as possible after death to prevent toxin degradation or clostridial overgrowth
  - Samples delivered directly or shipped overnight chilled (not frozen) on ice

- Positive culture for C. perfringens/difficile must be confirmed by identification of toxins/genes (non-pathogenic serovars are common)

**Zoonosis:** Information not found

**Note:** Mortality rate is high, especially with C. perfringens type C
