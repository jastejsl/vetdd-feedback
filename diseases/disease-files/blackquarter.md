**Caused by:** Clostridium chauvoei

**Can also be caused by:** C. novyi, C. septicum, C. sordellii

**Affects:** Cattle; Sheep (occasionally)

**Primary Organ System:** Musculoskeletal

**Clinical Signs:**

- Peracute/acute: sudden death most common first sign; fever, inappetence and ruminal stasis; progressive paralysis and severe lameness in affected muscle group (commonly shoulder, buttock or loin)

- Affected muscle swollen, emphysematous, crepitant with serohaemorrhagic exudate and rancid smell (rancid butter odour)

- Myocarditis (Harslag/sponssiekte): cardiac form with acute death; mortality ~100%

- Wound infections: localized swelling, emphysema and oedema (gas gangrene) from other clostridia

- Acute crepitant myonecrosis of large muscle groups, fever, sudden death

**Gross Pathology:**

- Gross: dark, emphysematous, foul-smelling muscle; pericardial fibrin; myocardial pallor or haemorrhage

- Gross (cardiac): fibrinous to serofibrinous pericarditis; haemorrhagic or pale, soft, swollen myocardium at the base of the heart

**Histology:**

- Histopathology: myonecrosis with large Gram-positive rods and gas vacuoles within muscle; minimal inflammatory infiltrate in peracute cases

- FAT (fluorescent antibody test) — at least 6 air-dried impression smears from lesions;

- Tissue blocks for culture (aseptic, anaerobic) and histopathology; blood smears ante-mortem

- Histology: coagulative myonecrosis with gas bubbles in cardiac and skeletal muscle

**Diagnosis:**

- Lab Findings — FAT & Culture

- Pathology: rapid putrefaction and bloat; spongy muscle with dark areas of necrosis; reddish, sweet-smelling rancid fluid in affected muscle; blood-stained frothy fluid from nose and anus; subcutaneous fluid accumulation and cellulitis; necrotic foci in myocardium; carcass condemned at abattoir

- Culture/smear: large Gram-positive rods from lesions under anaerobic conditions; fluorescent antibody test
