**Specific Causes:** Feline alphacoronavirus type 1

**Affects:** Domestic cats and wild felids

**Primary Organ System:** Peritoneal/Serosal; Vascular

**Secondary Organ System:** CNS; Ocular; Renal; Hepatic; Respiratory

**Transmission:** Faecal-oral; transient, intermittent shedders and carriers; FIP-inducing strains arise by mutation from FECV

**Clinical Signs:**

Acute:

- Effusive (wet form): ascites; hydrothorax; pericardial effusion; icterus; vomiting; diarrhoea; thickened intestines; pleural involvement (dyspnoea, coughing); fluctuating fever; loss of weight and appetite; shock

- Non-effusive (dry form): weight loss; anorexia; fluctuating fever; pyogranulomas in various organs; ophthalmic abnormalities (uveitis); CNS: paresis, convulsions, personality changes; renal: enlarged kidneys, proteinuria, uraemia

**Pathological signs:**

- Effusive: granular fibrinous exudate on serosal surfaces; thickened gelatinous mesentery; serofibrinous exudate in body cavities

- Non-effusive: CNS (granulomatous/fibrinous meningitis); ocular (anterior uveitis); renal (multifocal granulomatous nephritis); lungs (multifocal granulomatous pneumonia); hepatic (multifocal granulomatous hepatitis)

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Effusive (wet): strong humoral but no cellular immunity → type III hypersensitivity → complement-mediated necrotizing vasculitis and perivasculitis → fibrin-rich exudate → DIC

- Non-effusive (dry): strong humoral, weak cell-mediated immunity → pyogranulomatous inflammation → chronic lesions in organs

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Haematology: lymphopaenia; neutrophilia

- Serology; Rivalta test on effusion fluid

- Organ biopsy (only definitive test)

- PCR (cannot distinguish mutant from non-mutant forms)
