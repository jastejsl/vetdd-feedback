**Specific Causes:** Rift Valley fever virus (Phlebovirus; single-stranded RNA; bunyavirus family; bilipid envelope)

**Affects:** Sheep; goats; cattle; humans

**Primary Organ System:** Hepatic

**Secondary Organ System:** Reproductive; Vascular; Nervous

**Transmission:** Mosquitoes; outbreaks triggered by heavy rains/flooding; secondary vectors: Culex spp.; also via tissue contact/aerosol in humans

**Clinical Signs:**

Peracute:

- Newborns: fever; listlessness; abdominal pain; peracute death within 24–36 hours; mortality above 90%

Acute:

- Calves, older sheep/goats: fever; bloody diarrhoea; icterus; mortality 5–30%

- Adult cattle: may be inapparent; mortality \<10%

- Pregnant animals: abortion at any stage of gestation

**Pathological signs:**

- Hepatic: liver enlarged, soft, friable, yellowish-brown; necrotic foci

- Vascular: haemorrhages in abomasal mucosa

- Lymphoid: enlarged spleen with infarcts

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Mosquito bite → viraemia → hepatotropic infection → liver necrosis and icterus; in pregnant animals: viraemia reaches placenta → abortion; in newborns: overwhelms immune response → peracute death

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Virus isolation

- PCR (EDTA blood)

- Histopathology

- Serology (CF, ELISA, virus neutralisation)

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** yes

**Zoonosis:** yes

- Zoonosis: humans infected via tissue contact; moderate to severe flu-like illness; ocular lesions; haemorrhagic manifestations; encephalitis possible
