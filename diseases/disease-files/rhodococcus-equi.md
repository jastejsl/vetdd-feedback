**Caused by:** Rhodococcus equi

**Affects:** Foals 1–6 months (3 months most susceptible); horses.

Subacute to chronic abscessing bronchopneumonia (sometimes with ulcerative typhlocolitis); worldwide in long, hot summers; characteristic salmon-pink colour after a few days in culture

**Primary Organ System:** Respiratory

**Secondary Organ System:** Gastrointestinal; Lymphatic; Ophthalmic

**Clinical Signs:**

- Clinical signs may appear acute, but it is a chronic disease

- Anorexia, lethargy, fever, tachypnoea, weight loss

- Chronic suppurative bronchopneumonia and lymphadenitis

- Pneumonia

- Ulcerative lymphangitis, cellulitis, subcutaneous abscesses

- Abdominal disease (50% of pneumonic foals): Enterocolitis, typhlitis, abdominal abscesses, peritonitis

- Immune-mediated polysynovitis (1/3 of pneumonic cases)

- Hypopyon

**Pathological signs:**

- Lungs: Pyogranulomatous reaction; abscessing bronchopneumonia

- Lymph nodes: Lymphadenitis

- Intestinal tract: Ulcerative typhlocolitis (abdominal form)

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Inhalation of contaminated dust → pyogranulomatous reaction in regional lymph node → bacteria infects macrophages and survives intracellularly → persists in and destroys pneumonic macrophages → cellular immunity required (intracellular pathogen) → chronic suppurative bronchopneumonia; colonizes intestinal tract of foals passively → moves through intestine enhancing virulence

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Bacterial culture (characteristic salmon-pink colour)

- Cytological examination

- Radiology/ultrasound
