**Specific Causes:** *Clostridium botulinum* (botulinum neurotoxin types B, C, D)

**Affects:** Bovine, Porcine, Equine, Ovine, Caprine

**Primary Organ System:** Nervous

**Secondary Organ Systems:** Respiratory; Musculoskeletal

**Clinical Signs:**

- Afebrile, alert animals with ascending flaccid paralysis

- Progressive hindlimb weakness cranially; reduced tongue tone, dysphagia, drooling

- Ruminal/colonic stasis; constipation

- Lateral recumbency, death from respiratory paralysis; pica and osteophagia in cattle

- Sudden death

Horses: 

- Increasing inability to move

- Sternal recumbency

- Head resting

- GIT effects: Colic and constipation

- Difficulty/inability to drink/eat

- Death is due to respiratory paralysis

**Gross Pathology:**

- Gross: dehydration, constipation; foreign material in forestomachs

- Gross (CNS): no macroscopic CNS lesions — botulinum toxin cleaves SNARE proteins at the NMJ without causing structural CNS damage

- Secondary gross findings: denervation muscle atrophy in chronic cases; aspiration pneumonia secondary to dysphagia

**Diagnosis:**

- Feed / Tissue Toxicology

- Lab: toxin detection in serum, intestinal contents or feed via mouse bioassay or immunoassay

- History: access to bones, carrion, or poultry litter; phosphorus deficiency

Pathology of little value

**Vaccination regimen is available**
