**Specific Causes:** Equid alphaherpesvirus 1 (EHV-1; Varicellovirus equidalpha 1)

**A.K.A: **Equine Abortion / Neurological Disease

**Affects:** Horses (and other equids)

**Primary Organ System:** Reproductive; Respiratory; Nervous

**Secondary Organ System:** Cardiovascular (neonatal)

**Transmission:** Aerosol; fomites; inhalation; transplacental; infected aborted foetus/placenta as source

**Clinical Signs:**

- Abortion form: usually aborts 5–10th month of gestation; autolysed foetuses; no lesions on foetus

- Neonatal foal disease: weak foal born; rapid breathing; harsh lung sounds; death in 3–4 days

- Respiratory form (common in weanlings to 3-year-olds): congestion of nasal mucosa; serous rhinitis; conjunctivitis; atelectasis

- Neurological form: paralysis in adult horses (occasional)

**Pathological signs:**

- Respiratory: lungs firm and voluminous; atelectasis; submucosal haemorrhage in trachea and bronchi; intranuclear inclusions in lung cells

- Reproductive: placental oedema and congestion; foetal organs with focal necrosis

- CNS: encephalomyelitis due to microvasculature damage

- Cardiac: hydrothorax; collapsed lungs; petechiae in lungs (neonatal)

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Abortion form: infection via upper respiratory tract → replication in phagocytes (monocytes) → viraemia → bypasses placental barrier → foetal infection → abortion

- Neurological form: infection in endothelial cells of vasculature → damage to microvasculature of CNS → encephalomyelitis

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Virus isolation (brain, spinal cord, foetal lungs, nasopharynx swabs)

- Serology: ICF, IFA, serum neutralisation
