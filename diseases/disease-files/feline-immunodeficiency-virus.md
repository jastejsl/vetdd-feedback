**Specific Causes:** Feline immunodeficiency virus (5 subtypes: A, B, C, D, E)

**Affects:** Wild and domestic species of family Felidae only

**Primary Organ System:** Lymphoid/Immune

**Secondary Organ System:** Oral; Respiratory; Digestive; Nervous

**Transmission:** Mainly via biting wounds (saliva); transplacental (very rare); virus cannot survive outside host

**Clinical Signs**

Acute:

- Lymphadenopathy; fever; leukopenia

- Asymptomatic phase: lasts months to years

Chronic: 

- opportunistic infections of oral cavity, respiratory tract, digestive system, skin

- lethargy

- inappetence and weight loss

- pyrexia

- gingivitis

- diarrhoea

- rhinitis

- ocular discharge (uveitis)

- neoplasia in 25% of cases (lymphosarcomas)

- neurological: behavioural changes

- dementia

- convulsions

- naso-ocular discharge (‘snuffle appearance’)

**Pathological signs:**

- Lymphoid: depletion of CD4+ T lymphocytes; hypergammaglobulinaemia

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Virus infects T4-lymphocytes of regional lymph nodes → progressive spread to all lymph nodes → immunosuppression due to complete T4 (CD4+) depletion → susceptible to opportunistic infections; CD8+ cells play role in suppression of virus production after initial infection

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- ELISA snap test (p24 antigen/antibody) — highly sensitive; false positives in kittens \<6 months

- Antibody testing

- Clinical pathology: neutropenia/lymphopenia; hyperglobulinaemia; cytopenias
