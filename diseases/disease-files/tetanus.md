**Caused by:** Clostridium tetani

**Affects:** Horses and sheep; occasionally cattle, goats, pigs and humans

**Primary Organ System:** Nervous system

**Secondary Organ System:** Musculoskeletal system; Respiratory

**Transmission:** Source of C. tetani usually faeces, faecally contaminated environment, contaminated needles, nails, etc.; spores present in soil, dust; neonatal tetanus via infection of umbilical cord or lack of maternal immunity

**Clinical Signs:**

- Extended head

- Depression and dullness

- Raised, stiffly held tail

- Lockjaw

- Prolapse of third eyelids

- Sawhorse stance

- Marked stiffness

- Bloating

**Pathological signs:**

- No specific pathology found at post-mortem

- [Pending feedback]

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Spores enter wound → tissue necrosis and concurrent infection create a local REDOX potential predisposing to germination and growth of C. tetani → C. tetani remains localised at site of entry, producing neurotoxin (tetanospasmin) → tetanospasmin reaches CNS via humoral route (lymph/blood) or peripheral nerves → transported to nerve endings and reaches CNS via axons (intraneuronally) → presynaptic blockage of GABA and glycine at inhibitory neurons → constant muscular spasticity → sustained spasm of respiratory muscles → death by asphyxiation, cardiac arrest and general exhaustion

**Diagnosis****:**

- History and clinical signs

- Necropsy of little use

- Samples of necrotic wound tissue for C. tetani isolation

- Serum for toxin identification by toxin neutralisation tests

**Note:** Cattle with early tetanus respond to treatment better than most other livestock; not worth treating cattle with fully developed tetanus
