**Specific Causes:** Bacillus anthracis

**Affects:** Cattle; Sheep & Goats; Equine

**Primary Organ Systems:** Haematopoietic; Vascular

**Clinical Signs:**

- Acute (domestic ruminants): high fever, depression, digestive disturbances, some diarrhoea which may be haemorrhagic, petechiation of mucous membranes

- Peracute (wild ruminants): most found dead; blood-stained fluid from nostrils, mouth and anus

**Gross Pathology:**

- Gross (if opened in error or known safe): widespread serosal, mucosal and visceral petechiae and ecchymoses; dark, poorly clotted, tarry blood; blood-stained effusions into body cavities; spleen very enlarged, dark, soft in peracute cases

- Gross (Haematopoietic): massive splenomegaly — spleen 3–5× normal size, dark, friable, haemorrhagic; maroon-black, non-clotting blood.

- Gross (Cardiovascular): epicardial and endocardial haemorrhages; haemorrhagic pericardial effusion; DIC in terminal stages..

- Gross (Integument): cutaneous oedema and subcutaneous gelatinous haemorrhagic infiltration at natural orifices; blood-stained fluid from nostrils, mouth and anus.

**Histology:**

- Histology: large encapsulated rectangular Gram-positive bacilli in blood vessels and tissue; minimal inflammatory response (rapidly fatal before inflammatory reaction develops); acute splenitis with red pulp engorgement

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Mechanism: Anthrax spores ingested/inhaled → germinate and multiply → poly-D-glutamic acid capsule inhibits phagocytosis → anthrax toxin  → LF causes macrophage apoptosis and cytokine storm → overwhelming toxaemia → death

**Diagnosis:**

- Characteristic Gross Lesion

- DO NOT OPEN CARCASS — diagnose from blood smear from ear tip or tail; large encapsulated rectangular bacilli staining magenta-red with McFadyean's (polychrome methylene blue) stain

**Zoonosis:** Yes — zoonotic potential noted.

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** Yes
