**Specific Causes:** Bluetongue virus

**Affects:** Primarily sheep (spillover/most susceptible); cattle (amplifier host); goats; deer; African antelope

**Primary Organ System:** Integumentary; Vascular; Musculoskeletal

**Secondary Organ System:** Respiratory; Cardiovascular; Digestive

**Transmission:** Culicoides midges (biological vector); midges feed on cattle/antelope in spring/early summer and sheep in late summer; transplacental (with modified vaccine only)

**Clinical Signs:**

Acute:

- Sheep (mild): depression; fever; mild conjunctivitis; hyperaemia of nasal and labial mucosa; mild facial oedema

- Sheep (moderate): conjunctivitis; lacrimation; nasal discharge; facial oedema; coronitis

- Sheep (severe): respiratory distress; cyanosis of tongue (bluetongue); dyspnoea; torticollis

- Cattle (usually subclinical): fever; stiffness; lameness; lacrimation; salivation; swollen lips and tongue

**Pathological signs:**

- Oral/GIT: lesions and erosions of lips, oral mucosa; erosions and ulcers in stomach; hyperaemia of rumen

- Respiratory: severe lung oedema

- Cardiovascular: hydrothorax; hydropericardium; endocardial haemorrhage; necrosis of heart papillary muscles

- Musculoskeletal: skeletal muscle degeneration, haemorrhage and necrosis

- Coronary band: haemorrhage; coronitis

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- BTV infects regional lymph nodes → primary viraemia (mostly cell-associated with erythrocytes/leukocytes) → secondary viraemia causes vasculitis and thrombosis → ischaemic corium necrosis → coronitis and lameness; panleukopenia; lesions of stratified squamous epithelium

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Isolation of virus from spleen and lymph nodes

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** yes
