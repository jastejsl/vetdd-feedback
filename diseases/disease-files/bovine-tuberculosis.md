**Caused by:** Mycobacterium bovis

**Affects:** Cattle; Buffalo.

**Primary Organ System:** Respiratory

**Secondary Organ System****:** Gastrointestinal; Mammary gland; Lymphatics

**Transmission:** 

- Aerosol (direct, low dose)

- Ingestion (indirect, high dose)

- Percutaneous via oral cavity (direct, intraspecies aggression)

- Intra-uterine from dam (generalised bovine TB)

**Pathological signs:**

- Respiratory tract: 
  - Very small encapsulated, yellowish foci of caseous necrosis → coalesce and expand to form large areas of caseous bronchopneumonia; lesions may become calcified
  - Spread to visceral/parietal pleura causes granulomas to cluster and fuse ("grapes"), which may calcify ("pearl disease")

- Gastrointestinal: nodules or ulcers in mucosa of GIT

- Generalised tuberculosis: numerous small or large lesions in many organs and lymph nodes throughout the body

- Udder: four forms of tuberculous mastitis
  - Chronic organ TB (most lobules replaced by granulation tissue)
  - Disseminated miliary TB mastitis (small nodules cluster, caseate, calcify)
  - Caseous TB mastitis
  - Tuberculous galactophoritis

- Congenital TB: calves can contract infection from dams congenitally; primary complex occurs in liver and portal lymph nodes in the majority of cases

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:
**(vertically stacked)

Entry of bacilli → primary infection focus (e.g. alveolus, tonsil) 
→ ingestion by macrophages 
→ either destruction of bacilli OR establishment of tubercle 

→ If not arrested by immune response, tubercles grow in primarily affected organ(s), forming a caseous necrotic centre with possible liquefaction and calcification (can remain localised) 

Outcome depends on cell-mediated immunity, protective immune responses and hypersensitivity reactions, ranging from self-limiting to life-threatening systemic disease

Granuloma formation represents the host's attempt to localise infection and allow immune mechanisms to destroy bacilli

**Diagnosis****:**

- Post mortem: Direct detection with:
  - Macroscopic lesions
  - Histopathology
  - Microscopy (smears + tissue, not very sensitive)
  - Culture including PCR (confirmatory)

- Ante mortem: Direct detection via culture of bronchial and gastric lavage

- Indirect detection: 
  - Immuno-chromatographic test (rapid test, government prescribed)
  - Intradermal tuberculin test/skin test (delayed-type hypersensitivity test) — single intradermal test (bovine PPD only, cheaper, for screening) vs comparative intradermal test (bovine + avian PPD, more expensive, higher specificity)
  - Interferon-gamma assay (IFNg, ancillary test on heparinised blood)
  - ELISA (only suitable for detecting advanced stages)

**Notes on tests:**

- Intradermal tuberculin test: measurable increase in skin thickness, with or without oedema/necrosis at injection site, evaluated 72 hours after injection

- Non-specific reactors can occur due to M. avium complex, Johne's disease, skin lesions (dermatitis nodosa), other acid-fast organisms (e.g. Nocardia), environmental mycobacteria sensitisation, or external causes (insect/tick bites) resulting in false positives.

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** yes (Controlled disease; National BTB control scheme)
