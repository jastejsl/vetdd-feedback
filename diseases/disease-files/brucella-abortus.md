**Common names:** Contagious abortion (cattle brucellosis)

**Affects:** Cattle (dairy and beef); occasionally sheep, goats (abortion only, does not spread further); horses (localise in bursae, tendon sheaths, joints); buffalo (wildlife reservoir)

Cattle are the primary host; buffalo are considered a wildlife reservoir; controlled disease in SA; NB zoonosis causing undulant fever.

**Primary Organ System:** Reproductive system

**Secondary Organ System:** Synovial structures (tendovaginitis, bursitis, arthritis) 

**Transmission:** Ingestion of contaminated feed/water; contact with aborted foetus, foetal membranes and uterine discharge; calves infected in utero or via colostrum; bulls shed organisms in semen, seminal fluid and urine

**Clinical Signs:**

- Abortion at 7–9 months (cows usually abort once, on first pregnancy after contracting brucellosis)

- Retained placenta → risk of secondary infections

- Metritis (septicaemia)

- Decreased milk yield (milk appears normal)

- Stillbirths

- Weak calves

- Long-term: cows typically recover and have live offspring following initial abortion, but may continue to shed bacteria, especially around parturition

- Hygroma, especially of carpal joints

Bulls:

- Orchitis: necrotising

- Scrotal circumference may be severely enlarged

- Seminal vesiculitis

- Decreased libido (during acute phase only)

- Semen quality and appearance affected

**Pathological signs:**

- Placenta: intercotyledonary areas thickened (leathery) and oedematous

- Aborted foetus: subcutaneous oedema; blood-tinged fluid in thoracic and abdominal cavities; abomasal content bright yellow with whitish flakes (high numbers of brucella organisms); bronchopneumonia; enlarged liver

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Organisms penetrate mucous membranes → phagocytosed by neutrophils and macrophages → carried to regional lymph nodes → multiply → lymphadenitis → bacteraemia (may last months, resolve, or recur, particularly at parturition) → localise in organs, especially gravid uterus, udder and supramammary lymph nodes (affinity for erythritol, elevated in placenta/foetal fluids from 5th month of gestation) → chorionic epithelium parasitized, infection spreads to foetus → placentitis → abortion, premature birth, or birth of viable/non-viable calf

- Occasionally localises in synovial structures → tendovaginitis, bursitis or arthritis

**Diagnosis:**

- Culture to isolate B. abortus (aborted foetus lung & abomasal contents, placental & foetal membranes, supramammary/mandibular/retropharyngeal lymph nodes, testicular tissue)

- PCR (same samples as culture)

- Intradermal test (brucellin test): detects cellular immunity

- Lab findings

**Lab Findings**:

- Stamp's stain microscopy (resists decolorisation by weak acids, stains pink) — not confirmatory, used as a presumptive test

- Serology (indirect detection of antibodies): 
  - Rose Bengal test (screening, sensitive, low specificity)
  - Complement-fixation test (confirmatory post-RBT)
  - Slow (serum) agglutination test, iELISA & cELISA, Fluorescent polarisation assay

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** yes

**Zoonosis:** yes
