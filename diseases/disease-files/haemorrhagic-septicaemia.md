**Affects:** Cattle

**Primary Organ System:** Cardiovascular; Systemic (septicaemia)

**Secondary Organ System:** Lymphatic system; Respiratory

**Transmission:** 

- Aerosol or ingestion

- Predisposing factors: stress — sudden temperature drop, overcrowding, poor nutrition, excessive handling, excessive rainfall, concurrent viral/protozoal infection

**Clinical Signs:**

- High fever

- Dyspnoea

- Salivation

- Nasal discharge

- High mortality

**Pathological signs:**

- Widespread petechiae of serosal surfaces

- Ascites, hydrothorax, hydropericardium

- Lymph nodes: petechiae, oedema

- Severe generalised congestion

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Active carrier state: limited but ongoing multiplication of bacteria in tonsils and nasopharynx → shedding for 4–6 weeks; latent carrier state: arrest, no shedding

**Diagnosis:**

- Microscopic examination of blood/organ/bone marrow smears (taken shortly before death)

- Isolation of P. multocida

- Serotype-specific ELISA

- DNA typing

- PCR

**Note: **Control is difficult/impossible due to the peracute nature of the disease; sulfonamides, oxytetracycline, penicillin used in febrile animals (does NOT remove/prevent carrier state); maternal antibodies provide protection to calves for 45 days postpartum
