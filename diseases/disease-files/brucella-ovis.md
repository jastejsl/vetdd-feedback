**Common names:** Contagious epididymitis

**Affects:** Rams, ewes

Common cause of epididymitis in rams; low reproductive rates in affected flocks; rare cause of abortion in ewes; does not cause abortion as much as other Brucella species.

**Primary Organ System:** Reproductive system 

**Secondary Organ System****:** Lymph nodes; spleen; liver; kidneys; mammary gland

**Transmission:** Mainly spread by infected rams; ram-to-ram spread (excreted in semen intermittently for up to 4 years, sometimes throughout life; oral infection via sniffing/licking); venereal transmission (ram-ewe-ram), sporadic and transient

**Predisposing factors:** prevalence higher in flock rams than in stud rams (more regular examination of rams, individual vs mass mating, isolated rearing/housing of stud rams)

**Clinical Signs:**

- Breeding rams: reduced fertility or sterility

- Uni- or bilateral scrotal swelling, pain

- Enlargement of tail of epididymis

- Increased consistency; non-purulent inflammation

- Reduced mobility of testes in scrotum

- Testicular atrophy

- Poor semen quality

- Abortion and placentitis in ewes (rare)

**Pathological signs:**

- Genitalia: gross lesions only develop in genitalia (90% in tail of epididymis); epithelial necrosis with leakage of semen into interstitium; spermatic granulomas/spermatocoeles

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Infection via mucous membranes (oral, nasal, conjunctival, preputial, penile, vaginal mucosa) → bacteraemia → localisation in lymph nodes, urogenital tract, spleen, liver, kidneys → excreted in semen from 31–45 days post-infection → gross lesions develop in genitalia (90% tail of epididymis) → epithelial necrosis with leakage of semen to interstitium → spermatic granulomas/spermatocoeles

**Diagnosis:**

- Palpation (only successful if clinical disease present)

- Culture (B. ovis is intermittently excreted)

- Serology: CFT

**Lab Findings**:

- Semen smear shows neutrophils and intracellular, modified acid-fast bacteria (Stamp's stain)
