**Specific Causes:** African swine fever virus (only known DNA arbovirus)

**Affects:** Pigs; warthogs

**Primary Organ System:** Vascular; Lymphoid; Immune

**Secondary Organ System:** Respiratory; Digestive; Integumentary; Reproductive

**Transmission:** Sylvatic cycle: argasid ticks (Ornithodoros tampans) infect warthogs; domestic pig/tick cycle; pig-to-pig via oronasal route, fomites, and contaminated meat products

**Clinical Signs:**

Peracute:

- Found dead; recumbency

- High fever; flushing; huddling

- Rapid shallow breathing

Acute:

- Fever; flushed to cyanotic ears, lower legs, ventral abdomen

- Mucopurulent ocular and nasal discharge

- Abdominal pain; vomiting

- Constipation followed by blood-stained diarrhoea

- Ataxia

- Frothy blood-stained nasal/oral discharge

- Haemorrhages; abortions

- Mortality approaching 100%

Chronic:

- Emaciation; long dull hair coat

- Pneumonia; lameness; ulcers

**Pathological signs:**

- Vascular/Integumentary: congested conjunctival mucosa; cyanotic extremities and ventral surface; subcutaneous haemorrhages; pinpoint haemorrhages in renal cortex

- Lymphoid: enlarged haemorrhagic lymph nodes; enlarged spleen

- GIT: haemorrhagic stomach mucosa

- Respiratory: lungs do not collapse and are enlarged; straw or blood-coloured fluid in body cavities

- Chronic: fibrous pericarditis; fibrous epicarditis; interstitial pneumonia; enlarged fibrous lymph nodes

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Entry via tonsils and pharyngeal mucosa → mandibular and retropharyngeal lymph nodes → viraemia (associated with RBCs) → infection of monocytes, macrophages, and reticular cells → defects in immune system → widespread haemorrhages and effusions → DIC

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- PCR (EDTA blood)

- Virus isolation

- Antigen detection

- Histopathology and immunopathology

- Samples: blood/lymph nodes/spleen/tonsils on ice

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** yes
