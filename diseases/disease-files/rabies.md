**Specific Causes:** Rabies virus 

**Affects:** All warm-blooded mammals; humans

**Primary Organ System:** Nervous

**Secondary Organ System:** Salivary glands

**Transmission:** Mainly through bites; virus in saliva; scratches; mucosal contact; organ transplant; aerosol (rare)

**Clinical Signs:**

Acute (Furious rabies):

- Restlessness; nervousness; aggression

- Biting behaviour; depraved appetite

- Dysphagia; salivation

- Fixed stare; ataxia

- Convulsive seizures; paralysis; coma; death

Acute (Dumb/Paralytic form):

- Drooping jaw; dysphagia

- Paresis; paralysis; coma; death

- Cattle: thirsty but unable to swallow; continuous bellowing; salivation; wind-sucking; hind-quarter paralysis

**Pathological signs:**

- CNS: Negri bodies (intracytoplasmic inclusion bodies) in neurons; neuronal degeneration

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Virus enters via nerve endings of epithelium/subepithelium at bite site → enters nervous system via neuromuscular spindles and motor end plates → centripetal spread to CNS → centrifugal spread to salivary glands and dissemination throughout brain; immune response insufficient to clear virus

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Fluorescent antibody test (FAT — first-line test)

- Virus isolation

- Immunoperoxidase

- PCR

- Negri bodies on histopathology

**Notifiable / Controlled Disease:** yes

**Zoonosis:** yes

**Supplementary info:**

- Compulsory vaccination of dogs and cats: inactivated vaccine at 3 months, again within 12 months, then every 3 years; two biotypes in SA (Canid and Mongoose)
