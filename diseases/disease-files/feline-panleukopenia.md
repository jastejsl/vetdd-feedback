**Specific Causes:** Protoparvovirus carnivoran 1 (Feline parvovirus; same virus as CPV-2a and 2b)

**Affects:** All Felidae

**Primary Organ System:** Lymphoid/Immune; Digestive

**Secondary Organ System:** Nervous (cerebellar hypoplasia in foetal infection); Reproductive

**Transmission:** Direct contact; saliva, urine, vomitus, faeces; in utero; shed from all body secretions

**Clinical Signs:**

- Most cases subclinical; highest incidence in unvaccinated kittens 3–6 months

- Fever

- Depression

- Diarrhoea and/or vomiting

- Dehydration

- Panleukopenia

- Secondary bacterial infections

Acute:

- Cerebellar ataxia and intention tremors (infected in utero or during first 3 weeks of life)

- Early gestation: infertility and foetal resorption

- Mid gestation: abortion and mummification

- Late gestation/neonatal: cerebellar hypoplasia with jerky movements

**Pathological signs:**

- GIT: bowel loops firm and hyperaemic with petechial/ecchymotic haemorrhages

- Lymphoid: thymic atrophy

- CNS: cerebellar hypoplasia; hydrocephalus; hydranencephaly (congenitally infected)

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Initial replication in lymphoid tissue of oropharynx → viraemia → rapidly dividing cells (lymphoid tissue, crypts of Lieberkuhn, bone marrow stem cells) → lymphocyte depletion, panleukopenia, villous atrophy → secondary bacterial infections; in utero: cerebellum (most rapidly dividing) → cerebellar hypoplasia

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical Signs

- Pathology

- Haematology: marked panleukopenia

- Electron microscopy

- ELISA: direct antigen detection (PCR snap test)

- PCR

- Necropsy and histopathology
