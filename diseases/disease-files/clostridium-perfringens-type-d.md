**Common names:** Pulpy kidney; bloednier

**Affects:** Sheep (mostly young, less commonly goats); virtually unknown in cattle (individual cases reported in feedlot cattle and dairy goats)

One of the most important diseases of sheep in southern Africa and elsewhere; peracute, acute or chronic infectious enterotoxaemia of mostly young sheep; chronic form manifests as focal symmetric encephalomalacia (FSE). Is a saccharolytic pathogen.

**Primary Organ System:** Gastrointestinal, Nervous system (if chronic)

**Secondary Organ System:** Renal; Vascular (petechial haemorrhages); Respiratory

**Transmission:** 

- Can survive in soil for 6 months

- Occurs in low numbers in small intestine (especially ileum)
  - Requires predisposing factors to promote drastic increase in organisms and toxin production 
  - Overeating concentrates, sudden change from poor to lush pastures, grazing fodder crops, high protein diets
  - Deworming, coccidiosis, intestinal stasis (heavy tapeworm infestation)

**Clinical Signs:**

Peracute:

- Death within <2 hours

Acute:

- Death within 2–12 hours

- Loss of appetite, listlessness, depression, salivation, abdominal pain, abdomen often very hard and bloated

- Facial movements, frothing, hypersalivation, staggering gait, knuckling of fetlocks, ataxia, recumbence, terminal coma and rapid death

Chronic (<7 days):

- FSE acute neurological disorder form (stagger syndrome): unable to stand, lateral recumbency, appears blind, opisthotonus, rumen atonic

- FSE chronic form (dummy syndrome): loses fear of humans and dogs, aimless wandering, incoordination, paralysis of masseter muscles (inability to eat or drink), sternal recumbency, death in 7–14 days

Goats:

- Peracute: similar to sheep, sudden death

- Acute: profuse diarrhoea, abdominal discomfort, convulsions, recovery possible after 2–4 days

- Chronic: diarrhoea, progressive weight loss

**Pathological signs:**

- Kidney: mottled, soft, congested, haemorrhagic, cortex jelly-like or semi-fluid (rapid post-mortem autolysis)

- GIT: small intestine congested and distended with gas

- Lungs: congestion and oedema

- Pericardial sac: lots of fluid that clots on exposure to air

- Petechial haemorrhages in peritoneal surface, muscles of flanks, epicardium, diaphragm, thymus

- Liver: dark, congested with haemorrhagic spots

- Brain: FSE — bilateral symmetrical lesions in frontal lobes of white matter or thalamus and midbrain

- Goats: severe pulmonary oedema and fibrinous to fibrinonecrotic enterocolitis

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Sudden or excessive carbohydrate-rich diet → rumen microflora not adapted to converting starch / starch overload → excessive starch enters small intestine → C. perfringens type D multiplies in SI, producing large amounts of epsilon prototoxin (and alpha toxin) → epsilon prototoxin activated by trypsin in SI → epsilon toxin is endotheliotoxic, causing endothelial cell death, large pores in capillaries and increased permeability (increasing toxin absorption) → epsilon toxin binds to endothelium of capillaries of brain and other organs (kidneys, liver) → extracellular oedema develops in brain (vascular endothelium damage) → bilaterally symmetrical encephalomalacia mainly involving white matter (FSE)

**Diagnosis:**

- Sample collection: live animals — serum; dead animals (within 6 hours after death) — bacteriology (intestinal content of ileum and intestinal swabs, aseptic technique and anaerobic storage); histopathology (10% formalin) of brain and intestinal tissue

- Presumptive: history and clinical signs; smear microscopy; serology

- Confirmatory: isolation of agent and toxin typing (intestinal content); PCR typing of bacterium; histopathology (whole brain) — sharply demarcated focal areas of malacia (no inflammation but haemorrhages)

**Lab Findings**:

- Histopathology of whole brain shows sharply demarcated focal areas of malacia (no inflammation but haemorrhages), consistent with FSE

- Smear microscopy and serology used presumptively; PCR and toxin typing used confirmatorily

**Control:** vaccination with epsilon toxoid vaccine (pregnant ewes 4 weeks before lambing; lambs 2–3 months of age) — natural immunity is not sufficient as clostridia are poor inducers of natural immunity
