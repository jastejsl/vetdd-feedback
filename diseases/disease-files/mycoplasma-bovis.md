**Affects:** Cattle (dairy and calves)

**Primary Organ System:** Respiratory

**Secondary Organ System:** Mammary gland; Musculoskeletal; Ear

**Transmission:** 

- Introduction of asymptomatically infected animals into herd

- Aerosols, nose-to-nose contact, or indirectly via drinking water

- Drenching and balling guns can spread disease between calves

- Dairy cattle: contagious mastitis pathogen with udder-to-udder spread; calves: ingestion of infected milk

**Clinical Signs:**

Mastitis:

- Swollen but not usually painful mammary gland with brownish secretions

- More than one quarter affected

- Drastic decrease in milk production

- Mild systemic illness

Pneumonia:

- Non-specific: fever, tachypnea, dyspnea, decreased appetite, nasal discharge and coughing

- Poor weight gain

- Accompanied by cases of otitis media and arthritis

- CPPS (chronic pneumonia and polyarthritis syndrome): animals develop polyarthritis in association with chronic pneumonia

Otitis media:

- Ear droop and ptosis

- Head shaking and scratching or rubbing ears

- Purulent aural discharge if tympanic membrane ruptures

- Advanced otitis media-interna; meningitis can develop

Arthritis:

- Develops 2–4 weeks after onset of pneumonia

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism:**

- Colonises mucosal surfaces of upper respiratory tract mucosa → persists and attaches to mucosal surfaces, evading host immunity → exposed membrane proteins form primary interface with host → phospholipases, hydrogen peroxide and superoxide radicals damage host tissue

**Diagnosis:**

- Suspected when joint swelling or single drooped ears are noted in a group of calves that have had pneumonia

- Definitive diagnosis difficult

- Sandwich ELISA (sELISA) kit

**Notes:**

- High economic impact: reduced production, loss of weight and carcass value
