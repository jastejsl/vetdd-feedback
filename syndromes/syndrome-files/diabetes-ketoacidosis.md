**Affects:** Dog ; Cat

**Primary Organ System:** Endocrine

**Secondary Organ System:** Renal ; Cardiovascular ; GIT ; Neuromuscular

**Predisposition:** Any diabetic animal

**Clinical Signs:**

- Anorexia and vomiting

- Severe lethargy, weakness, and depression

- Severe dehydration

- Kussmaul breathing (deep, laboured — respiratory compensation for metabolic acidosis)

- Ketone/acetone smell on breath

- Abdominal pain

Acute/Critical:

- Collapse and hypovolaemic shock

- Obtundation to coma (severe acidosis)

- Oliguria/anuria (hypovolaemic shock → acute renal failure)

**Pathological signs:**

- Liver: severe hepatic lipidosis (massive lipolysis)

- Kidneys: acute tubular necrosis (hypovolaemia-induced ischaemia)

- GIT: gastric erosions and haemorrhage

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism of development:**

- Absolute or relative insulin deficiency + stressor → counter-regulatory gluconeogenesis hormone surge → rapid, unrestrained lipolysis → massive free fatty acid liberation → hepatic ketogenesis → accumulation of ketone bodies → high anion gap metabolic acidosis

- Ketones also act as osmotic diuretic → osmotic diuresis on top of glucose-induced diuresis → severe dehydration + electrolyte losses (Na⁺, K⁺, PO₄³⁻)

- Paradox of potassium: total body K⁺ depleted (urinary loss) but serum K⁺ may appear normal or elevated initially (acidosis drives K⁺ out of cells in exchange for H⁺) → dangerous hypokalaemia develops once insulin therapy is started (K⁺ shifts back intracellularly) — must supplement K⁺ during treatment

- Metabolic acidosis + hypovolaemia → decreased cardiac output → death from combined acidosis and hypovolaemic shock if untreated

- Somogyi over-swing: excess insulin → hypoglycaemia → counter-regulatory hormone release → rebound hyperglycaemia → clinician erroneously increases insulin dose → cycle worsens; correct action is to reduce insulin dose

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical signs + history of diabetic-like symptoms

- Lab findings

- Identification of precipitating cause: urine culture, thoracic radiography, abdominal US, ACTH stimulation test if Cushings suspected

**Lab findings:**

- Blood glucose: severe persistent hyperglycaemia

- Urinalysis: glucosuria + ketonuria (ketonuria can precede ketonaemia in early stages)

- Blood gas/acid-base: metabolic acidosis (↓pH, ↓HCO₃⁻, ↓base excess, compensatory ↓pCO₂) with compensatory respiratory alkalosis

- Serum electrolytes: hypokalaemia and hypophosphataemia (post-treatment risk); initial K ⁺  may be normal or elevated

- Serum biochemistry: azotaemia (pre-renal), elevated ALP + ALT (hepatic lipidosis), hypercholesterolaemia

- Haematology: neutrophilia with left shift — confirm and identify precipitating infection
