**Affects:** Dog ; Cat

**Primary Organ System:** Endocrine (adrenal cortex — zona fasciculata/reticularis)

**Secondary Organ System:** Integument ; Liver ; Musculoskeletal ; Urinary ; Reproductive ; Cardiovascular

**Predisposition:** 

- Middle-aged to older dogs (6y< PDH; 9y< ADH)

- Small breeds for PDH (poodles, dachshunds, terriers)

- Large breeds for ADH

- Females for ADH

- Cats with concurrent DM

**Clinical Signs:**

- Polyuria and polydipsia (PUPD) — direct glucocorticoid effect; often first sign

- Polyphagia — direct glucocorticoid effect

- Pot-bellied/pendulous abdomen — abdominal fat redistribution and abdominal muscle catabolism

- Bilateral symmetrical truncal alopecia — non-pruritic unless secondary pyoderma

- Thin, inelastic, hyperpigmented skin; calcinosis cutis; phlebectasias; comedones near prepuce

- Thick neck / dorsal cervical fat pad

- Muscle weakness, lethargy, exercise intolerance, lordosis, braced posture, temporal muscle atrophy

- Panting — intercostal muscle weakness + increased intra-thoracic fat + diaphragm pressure

- Testicular atrophy; failure to cycle (FSH suppression by cortisol)

- Susceptibility to 2° bacterial infections — skin, UTI, conjunctiva, lungs (immunosuppression)

- Hypertension (50% of cases) → risk of retinal detachment, proteinuria, thromboembolic disease

**Pathological signs:**

- Adrenals: bilateral cortical hyperplasia (PDH) OR unilateral tumorous enlargement + contralateral atrophy (ADH)

- Liver: steroid hepatopathy — vacuolar hepatopathy (fat and glycogen in hepatocytes), hepatomegaly

- Skin: epidermal atrophy, loss of collagen/elastin, dermal mineralisation (calcinosis cutis), bilaterally symmetrical alopecia

- Lymphoid tissue: atrophy of lymph nodes, spleen, thymus

- Skeletal muscle: atrophy and wasting of appendicular and abdominal musculature

- Lungs/stomach wall/skeletal muscle: mineralisation from calcium salt deposition in collagen/elastin

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism of development:**

- PDH (80–90%): ACTH-secreting chromophobe adenoma/carcinoma of pituitary (resistant to –ve FB) → chronic excess ACTH stimulation → bilateral adrenocortical hyperplasia → chronic cortisol excess → gluconeogenesis↑, lipolysis↑ (centripetal fat redistribution), protein catabolism (muscle wasting, thin skin), anti-inflammatory/immunosuppressive effect → all clinical signs follow

- ADH (10–15%): Functional adrenocortical adenoma/carcinoma secretes cortisol independently of ACTH → strong –ve FB suppresses ACTH/CRH → contralateral adrenal atrophy → same metabolic consequences as PDH

- Iatrogenic: Chronic exogenous glucocorticoid admin → bilateral adrenal atrophy + diminished reserve (mimics Addisons on ACTH stim)

**Diagnosis:**

- ACTH stimulation test — confirms hyperadrenocorticism
  - Post-stim cortisol >600 nmol/L consistent with CCS
  - Iatrogenic = hypostimulation

- LDDST — cortisol fails to suppress at 8h (<30 nmol/L)
  - Confirms CCS but does not differentiate PDH vs ADH

- HDDST — suppression in PDH but not ADH

- Endogenous ACTH: high in PDH; low/undetectable in ADH

- Ultrasound: bilateral adrenomegaly (PDH) vs unilateral tumour + contralateral atrophy (ADH; normal <8mm)

- CT/MRI: pituitary tumour visualisation in PDH

**Lab findings:**

- Serum biochemistry: 
  - Elevated ALP (steroid-induced isoenzyme)
  - Elevated ALT
  - Hypercholesterolaemia (90%)
  - Stress leukogram
  - Mild erythrocytosis

- Urinalysis: 
  - Dilute urine (SG <1.020)
  - Glucosuria if concurrent DM
  - Bacteriuria without pyuria possible

- Urine cortisol : creatinine ratio (UCR) — sensitive screening test
  - High ratio warrants further testing
  - Normal ratio effectively rules out Cushings
