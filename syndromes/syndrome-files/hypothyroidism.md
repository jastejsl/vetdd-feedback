**Affects:** Dog ; Cat (rare) ; Horse (congenital)

**Primary Organ System:** Endocrine

**Secondary Organ System:** Integument ; Neuromuscular ; Cardiovascular ; Reproductive ; GIT

**Predisposition:** Middle-aged large breed dogs; mean age 7y; peak incidence 4–6y.

**Predisposed breeds:** Boxer, Golden Retriever, Doberman Pinscher, Irish Setter, Dachshund, Poodle; either sex (possibly females slightly more); rare in cats

**Clinical Signs:**

Metabolic signs:

- Dull, depressed, lethargic — most common presenting mental status change

- Weight gain without increased appetite

- Heat-seeking; cold intolerance

- Exercise intolerance

Dermatological signs:

- Bilateral symmetrical non-pruritic alopecia @ trunk, pressure points, "rat tail"

- Dry, lusterless coat

- Seborrhoea

- Skin thickening; "tragic facial expression" — myxoedema

- Clipped areas fail to regrow; poor wound healing; bruising

- Secondary bacterial skin infections (2° immunosuppression)

Neuromuscular signs:

- Peripheral neuropathy (vestibular signs, unilateral facial nerve paralysis, limb lameness)

- Myopathy — stiff stilted gait, weakness, hind limb dragging/scuffing of toenails, type II fibre atrophy

Cardiac signs:

- Bradycardia, weak apical impulse and pulse, low voltage ECG

- Reduced fractional shortening (myocardial hypokinesis)

Reproductive signs:

- Reproductive failure; hyperprolactinaemia (lactation in intact bitches via elevated TRH)

Other:

- Constipation (prolonged GI transit time; pathognomonically dry faeces)

- Congenital hypothyroidism (cretinism): disproportionate dwarfism (broad head, short legs, wide trunk), growth retardation, mental dullness, oedematous face

**Pathological signs:**

- Thyroid gland: 
  - Lymphocytic infiltration and fibrosis (lymphocytic thyroiditis) 
  - Follicular atrophy with loss of follicular epithelium (idiopathic follicular atrophy)

- Skin: 
  - Orthokeratotic hyperkeratosis
  - Follicular keratosis
  - Follicular atrophy
  - Mucin deposition in dermis (myxoedema)

- Skeletal muscle: type II fibre atrophy

- Nerves: mucopolysaccharide accumulation in peri- and endoneurium → pressure on peripheral nerves

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism of development:**

- Primary: 
Immune-mediated lymphocytic thyroiditis OR idiopathic follicular atrophy 
→ progressive destruction of thyroid follicular cells 
→ ↓T4 and T3 secretion → ↓negative feedback on hypothalamus/pituitary 
→ ↑TRH and ↑TSH (TSH elevated = primary hypothyroid hallmark) 
→ thyroid hormones affect ALL tissues: ↓oxidative metabolism/BMR 
→ weight gain, cold intolerance, lethargy, bradycardia, constipation + ↑TRH 
→ hyperprolactinaemia → lactation in intact bitches ± altered connective tissue metabolism 
→ mucopolysaccharide accumulation → myxoedema

- Euthyroid Sick Syndrome (ESS):
Concurrent non-thyroidal disease (Cushings, DM, liver/cardiac/renal disease) 
→ profoundly lowers baseline TT4 despite normal thyroid function 
→ mimics hypothyroidism - TT4 below normal but T4 supplementation is CONTRA-INDICATED
  - ESS must be excluded before diagnosing hypothyroidism


**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical signs + signalment (middle-aged large breed dog; bilateral symmetrical alopecia; weight gain; lethargy; bradycardia)

- ECG: bradycardia; low voltage complexes; reduced fractional shortening on echocardiography

**Lab findings:**

- Low serum total T4 (TT4) - highly sensitive screening test but poor specificity due to ESS
  - NEVER diagnose hypothyroidism on single TT4 result alone

- Elevated endogenous TSH in primary hypothyroidism: combined low TT4 + high TSH (”confirmatory”)
  -  Note: some hypothyroid dogs have TSH within reference range (pituitary exhaustion; concurrent glucocorticoid therapy; 2° hypothyroidism; hormone fluctuation)

- Serum free T4 (fT4) by equilibrium dialysis: best discriminates hypothyroidism from ESS (not yet available in SA)

- Haematology: mild normocytic normochromic non-regenerative anaemia; no classic WBC changes

- Serum biochemistry: fasting hypercholesterolaemia (75% — high index of suspicion)

- Occasional elevated ALP, ALT; elevated CK (myopathy)
