**Affects:** Dog ; Cat

**Primary Organ System:** Endocrine

**Secondary Organ System:** Liver ; Renal ; Ocular ; Neuromuscular ; Cardiovascular

**Predisposition:** Middle-aged to older dogs and cats; obese animals; concurrent Cushings, pancreatitis, or corticosteroid/progestogen therapy

**Breed predispositions:** Miniature Schnauzers, Fox Terriers, Poodles, Collies, Samoyeds, Tibetan Terriers, Cairn Terriers, Rottweilers. 

**Clinical Signs:**

- Classic four: polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, and weight loss

- Lethargy and depression

- Cataracts — dogs (glucose deposition in lens fibres → osmotic damage)

- Plantigrade posture in cats — diabetic peripheral neuropathy (hocks touching ground)

- Recurrent bacterial infections (immunosuppression from chronic hyperglycaemia)

DKA = severe stage, see linked card above.

**Pathological signs:**

- Liver: hepatic lipidosis; hepatomegaly

- Islets of Langerhans: amyloid deposition (cats); β-cell destruction/loss

- Skeletal muscle: atrophy due to gluconeogenesis from amino acids

- Kidneys: glomerular changes with chronicity

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism of development:
**(stacked vertically)

- Type 1 (Insulin Dependent DM): 

Immune-mediated destruction / acute pancreatic necrosis / amyloidosis of islets 
→ absolute insulin deficiency 
→ uninhibited glucagon release 
→ unrestrained hepatic glucose production 
→ persistent hyperglycaemia 
→ glucosuria 
→ osmotic diuresis (polyuria/polydipsia)

↓insulin → gluconeogenesis from amino acids 
→ skeletal muscle atrophy

↑lipolysis → hyperlipidaemia 
→ hepatic lipidosis

glucose toxicity + lipotoxicity further suppress remaining β-cells; hypokalaemia (K ⁺ lost in urine with glucose)

- Type 2 (Non-Insulin Dependent DM — especially cats):
 
Obesity → insulin resistance + relative β-cell dysfunction 
→ persistent hyperglycaemia

glucose toxicity + lipotoxicity further impair β-cells 
→ eventual progression to IDDM state

 reversible if caught early (some cats achieve diabetic remission)


- Diabetogenic hormones (progesterone, corticosteroids, GH, glucagon) → impaired insulin action / insulin resistance and β-cell suppression → secondary DM

**Diagnosis:**

- Documentation of persistent fasting hyperglycaemia (establishes diagnosis); fructosamine measurement useful in cats to distinguish from stress hyperglycaemia

- Cataracts (dogs) and plantigrade posture (cats) — important clinical clues

**Lab findings:**

- Urinalysis: glucosuria (consistent finding); ketonuria (DKA); bacteriuria (common)

- Serum biochemistry:
  - Elevated ALP and ALT (hepatic lipidosis)
  - Hypercholesterolaemia
  - Azotaemia possible
  - DKA worsens (look at card)

- Haematology: usually unremarkable
  - Neutrophilia with left shift → concurrent infection (common DKA precipitant)

- Pancreatic enzymes elevated: 
  - Serum amylase
  - Lipase
  - Pancreatic-specific lipase if concurrent pancreatitis suspected

- T4 levels: may be low in poorly controlled diabetics (euthyroid sick syndrome)
