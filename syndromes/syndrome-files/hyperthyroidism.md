**Affects:** Cat ; Dog (rare)

**Primary Organ System:** Endocrine

**Secondary Organ System:** Cardiovascular ; GIT ; Musculoskeletal ; Renal ; Integument

**Predisposition:** Cats >8 years old; no clear sex predisposition; hyperthyroidism in dogs usually associated with functional thyroid carcinoma; concurrent CKD common in cats (GFR elevation masks CKD while hyperthyroid)

**Clinical Signs:**

- Weight loss despite normal to increased appetite (polyphagia) — increased metabolic rate

- Polyuria and polydipsia

- Hyperactivity, restlessness, irritability

- Vomiting and/or diarrhoea

- Palpable enlarged thyroid gland

- Heat intolerance; skin warm to touch

- Soft, silky hair coat

Acute/Thyroid Storm (rare):

- Tachycardia and tachyarrhythmias

- Hypertension (systolic BP >160 mmHg)

- Left ventricular hypertrophy → congestive heart failure

- Hyperventilation

**Pathological signs:**

- Thyroid gland: unilateral or bilateral nodular hyperplasia or functional adenoma (multinodular); carcinoma rare

- Cardiovascular: left ventricular hypertrophy (concentric)

- Skeletal muscle: atrophy from increased catabolic metabolic rate and proteolysis

**Pathophysiology / Mechanism of development:**

- Primary hyperthyroidism (most common in cats): 

Functional thyroid adenoma/toxic multinodular goitre OR autoimmune 
→ autonomous production of T3 + T4 independent of TSH 
→ thyroid hormones act on ALL tissues → ↑oxidative metabolism in all cells 
→ ↑BMR → weight loss despite polyphagia, heat production, heat intolerance + ↑cardiac β₁ receptor sensitivity 
→ tachycardia, LV hypertrophy, hypertension/ + ↑GIT motility 
→ diarrhoea + polyphagia & ↑renal blood flow 
→ GFR appears elevated (masking concurrent CKD — CRITICAL: treating hyperthyroidism may unmask and worsen CKD)


- Secondary hyperthyroidism (rare): 
TSH/TRH-secreting pituitary adenoma → excess TSH → thyroid stimulation → excess T3/T4


- High T3/T4 → suppressed TSH (negative feedback on pituitary/hypothalamus) → hallmark biochemical finding

**Diagnosis:**

- Clinical signs + signalment (old cat, weight loss + polyphagia + hyperactivity + palpable thyroid)

- Blood pressure: hypertension common

- Thoracic radiography / echocardiography: cardiac changes (LV hypertrophy, cardiomegaly, pulmonary oedema)

- Thyroid scintigraphy (nuclear scan): functional thyroid tissue mapping — useful pre-surgery or radioiodine therapy

**Lab findings:**

- Elevated total T4 (TT4): best initial screening test
  - Concurrent severe non-thyroidal illness (ESS) can lower T4 even in a truly hyperthyroid cat

- Serum free T4 (fT4) by equilibrium dialysis: more sensitive and specific
  - Useful when TT4 is equivocal

- Lower endogenous TSH: confirms hyperthyroidism if other signs present too

- Serum biochemistry: 
  - Elevated ALP and ALT
  - Elevated urea + creatinine  (Azotaemia)
    - Rule out concurrent CKD before initiating treatment
